Low-voltage motor common faults and solutions

  • Friday, 15 August 2025
  • 0
  • 179
  • 0

As an indispensable power equipment in industrial production and civil equipment, low-voltage motors are widely used in water pumps, fans, conveyor belts and other scenarios due to their simple structure and convenient operation. However, in long-term operation, low-voltage motors inevitably due to environmental, operational or aging problems such as failure, timely investigation and treatment to ensure the normal operation of the equipment is critical.


One of the most common failures of low-voltage motors is overheating. If the low voltage motor shell temperature rises abnormally in operation, even with the odor, mostly due to overload, poor heat dissipation or winding short circuit. At this time, you need to stop immediately to check: first check whether the load exceeds the rated power of the low-voltage motor, reduce unnecessary loads; and then clean up the dust and oil on the heat sink to ensure smooth ventilation; if still overheating, you need to test the insulation resistance of the windings, replace the damaged winding coils, to avoid overheating and burn the low-voltage motor.

Rattling is also a frequent problem of low-voltage motors. If the low voltage motor emits “buzzing” noise or metal friction sound during operation, it may be caused by bearing wear, rotor and stator friction, or loose fan blades. To solve the problem, you need to disassemble the end cover of the low voltage motor, check whether the bearings are out of oil or damaged, and then add grease or replace the bearings with the same type; if the gap between the rotor and the stator is abnormal, you need to calibrate the position of the rotor; if the fan blade is loose, then you need to tighten the screws to prevent the low voltage motor from being damaged due to vibration.

Starting failure is an “emergency” that affects the normal operation of low-voltage motors. After pressing the start button, the low-voltage motor is unresponsive or only slightly vibrating, mostly related to power supply failure, contactor damage or winding disconnection. You should first check whether the supply voltage is normal, whether the line is loose or broken; then test whether the contactor contacts are oxidized, if necessary, grinding or replacing the contactor; if the winding is broken, you need to use a multimeter to detect the location of the broken wire, and then re-weld or replace the winding, to ensure that the low-voltage motor circuit is smooth.

In addition, the insulation failure of the low-voltage motor should not be ignored. Wet environment or long-term idle, low-voltage motor winding insulation layer is easy to aging damage, resulting in leakage or short circuit. Daily need to keep the low-voltage motor working environment dry, regular insulation resistance meter to detect the insulation resistance, if the value is lower than the standard, the winding needs to be dried, or re-impregnated with paint insulation, to avoid safety accidents caused by insulation failure.

In short, the stable operation of low-voltage motors can not be separated from routine maintenance and timely maintenance. Mastering the judgment and solution of common failures can effectively extend the service life of low-voltage motors, to ensure the smooth progress of production and life.
